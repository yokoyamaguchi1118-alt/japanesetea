Starting with Japanese tea can feel overwhelming — there are dozens of
grades, regions, and brewing methods before you've even tasted anything.
This list skips the deep end and ranks the five teas we'd actually hand
to someone ordering for the first time.

## 1. Sencha

Grassy, a little sweet, and forgiving to brew. Sencha is the tea most
households in Japan drink every day, which makes it the safest and most
representative place to start.

## 2. Hojicha

Roasted rather than steamed, hojicha is naturally low in caffeine and
almost impossible to over-steep. If sencha's grassiness isn't for you,
this is the next stop.

## 3. Genmaicha

Sencha blended with roasted rice — nutty, comforting, and easy to drink
without sugar or milk.

## 4. Matcha

More involved to prepare, but worth learning early if you want the
ceremonial side of Japanese tea rather than just the everyday cup.

## 5. Gyokuro

Save this one for last. Shade-grown and umami-heavy, gyokuro rewards
palates that already know what sencha tastes like — which is exactly why
it's ranked here rather than first.
