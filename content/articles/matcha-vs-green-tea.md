Matcha and sencha both come from *Camellia sinensis*, the same plant
behind every other true tea. What separates them isn't the plant — it's
how the leaves are grown, processed, and consumed.

## Shade-growing

Matcha leaves (tencha before grinding) are shaded for several weeks
before harvest. Less direct sunlight increases chlorophyll and amino
acids like L-theanine, which is where matcha's deep color and umami
sweetness come from.

## Whole leaf vs. steeped leaf

With sencha, you steep the leaves and drink the liquid, discarding the
leaf afterward. With matcha, the entire leaf is stone-ground into a fine
powder and whisked directly into the water — you're drinking the whole
leaf, not an infusion of it.

## What that changes in the cup

Because you consume the whole leaf, matcha delivers more caffeine and
nutrients per serving than a steeped tea like sencha. It's also more
textured — thicker, slightly frothy, and more intense — where sencha is
lighter and more refreshing.

## Which one should you start with?

If you want an easy, everyday cup, start with sencha. If you want the
fuller, more ceremonial experience — and don't mind the extra equipment
— matcha is worth learning early.
