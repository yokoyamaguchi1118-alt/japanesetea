Sencha (煎茶) is the tea most people in Japan drink without thinking
about it — the default cup at home, at a restaurant, or from a vending
machine. Understanding it is the fastest way to understand Japanese
green tea in general.

## How it's made

Unlike Chinese green teas, which are typically pan-fired, sencha leaves
are steamed shortly after picking. Steaming stops oxidation quickly and
locks in a bright, grassy, slightly vegetal flavor that's distinct from
other green teas.

## What it tastes like

Expect grassiness, a light sweetness, and a clean, brisk finish. Lower
grades lean more bitter and astringent; higher grades are rounder and
sweeter, with more umami.

## How to brew it

- Water: 70–80°C (not boiling — boiling water pulls out excess
  bitterness)
- Leaf: about 2g per 150ml
- Steep: 60–90 seconds for the first infusion

Sencha leaves can usually be steeped two or three times, with each
infusion revealing a slightly different side of the tea.
